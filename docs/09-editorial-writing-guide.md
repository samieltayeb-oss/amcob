## 1. Core Voice and Perspective

AMCOB speaks with the quiet confidence of an established business institution. Our voice sounds like a thoughtful person who knows the community, understands the demands of running an enterprise, and respects the reader's time.

We do not sound like a corporate brochure, an advertising agency, or an automated AI assistant. Every sentence should feel grounded, human, and direct.

### Core Principles
1. **Plainspoken Authority:** Speak directly about business realities. Founders and executives face real challenges including hiring, cash flow, governance, and loneliness at the top. Address those realities without melodrama.
2. **Community Grounding:** Reflect the values of ethical stewardship, mutual support, and communal consultation (*Shura*) without preaching or inventing doctrinal claims.
3. **Clarity Over Grandeur:** Explain what people can actually do, who a program is for, and what happens next. Do not replace useful information with grandiose promises.

## 2. Mandatory Writing Rules

### 2.1 Punctuation and Dash Prohibition
- **Do not use em dashes (—), en dashes (–), or hyphens (-) to connect phrases in prose.**
- Use complete sentences, commas, colons, or full stops instead.
- **Allowed exceptions:**
  - Standard numerical ranges (such as $1M to $5M or October 26 through 30, though writing "to" and "through" in words is preferred in prose).
  - Technical identifiers, code symbols, filenames, and URLs (such as `concept-studio.html` or `my.amcob.org`).
  - Official hyphenated proper names (such as "B2B" or "co-founder").

### 2.2 Prohibited Buzzwords and Promotional Clichés
Never use generic marketing slogans or corporate filler. The following phrases are explicitly barred across all pages, forms, emails, and interface microcopy:

| Barred Phrase | Why It Fails | Approved Replacement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "Unlock your potential" | Empty self-help cliché | "Build operational discipline with peers who run similar enterprises." |
| "Elevate your journey" | Vague and condescending | "Address the specific challenges of scaling past $5M in revenue." |
| "Seamless ecosystem" | Meaningless tech buzzword | "Access roundtables, member directories, and international delegations." |
| "Transformative experience" | Inflated claim | "Confidential monthly meetings led by a trained facilitator." |
| "Where ambition meets opportunity" | Generic marketing formula | "A private peer group for Muslim business owners and chief executives." |
| "Sovereign global flagship" | Pretentious and institutional | "A trusted business community across North America and international markets." |
| "Unlock your future" | Vague call to action | "Explore membership" or "Review eligibility criteria" |

### 2.3 Structural and Syntactical Variety
- **Vary sentence length naturally:** Pair short, definitive statements with balanced, explanatory sentences.
- **Avoid formulaic repetition:** Do not structure every card or paragraph with identical patterns.
- **Avoid three-word slogans:** Phrases like "Connect. Grow. Lead." or "Inspire. Empower. Elevate." sound synthetic and cheapen the institution.
- **Avoid "not just X, but Y" constructions:** State what something is directly rather than setting up false rhetorical contrasts.
- **Avoid excessive adjectives:** Eliminate qualifiers like "unprecedented," "world-class," "premier," "revolutionary," and "bespoke." Let facts demonstrate quality.

### 2.4 Clear, Meaningful Interface Microcopy
Button and navigation labels must inform the user exactly what will happen when clicked:

- **Prefer:** "Explore membership", "Review peer groups", "Check tier eligibility", "View calendar", "Find a business profile", "Submit application".
- **Reject:** "Begin your journey", "Get started", "Join the movement", "Step inside", "Learn more".

## 3. Factual Integrity and Baseline Preservation

1. **Preserve Official Names:** Always use verified tier names (Emerging Enterprises, Booming Enterprises, Scaling Enterprises, Summit, Powerhouse) and official initiative names (AMCOB Connect, LEAD Summit, Khadija Collective, AMCOB Tours).
2. **Never Invent Proof:** Do not invent quotes, fictitious members, synthetic case studies, or fabricated revenue numbers. If an asset or quote is unverified, flag it clearly as provisional.
3. **Transparent Criteria:** State revenue thresholds, meeting formats, and fee structures plainly so applicants understand where they fit before applying.

## 4. Before-and-After Copy Transformations

```
BEFORE (Inflated / Clichéd):
"Welcome to AMCOB — a seamless ecosystem where visionary Muslim leaders unlock their potential and elevate their journey through transformative peer circles."

AFTER (Approved Standard):
"Running a growing company brings unique challenges, especially when you carry the responsibility alone. AMCOB brings together Muslim founders, chief executives, and investors in private peer advisory groups. Members meet monthly to discuss strategy, governance, and business decisions in complete confidence."
```

```
BEFORE (Dash-heavy / Jargon):
"AMCOB Connect — our revolutionary AI-powered matching engine — bridges your enterprise with trusted commercial partners across the globe."

AFTER (Approved Standard):
"AMCOB Connect helps members find verified commercial partners across the United States, Canada, and overseas markets. The portal matches your business needs with fellow member companies across key industry sectors."
```

```
BEFORE (Vague Call to Action):
"Begin your journey into the premier circle of excellence."

AFTER (Approved Standard):
"Explore membership tiers and check eligibility requirements."
```

## 5. Read-Aloud Protocol and Sign-Off

Before any page or component is approved for design or implementation, the author must read the copy aloud. If a sentence sounds stiff, theatrical, or unlikely to be spoken by a sensible business owner over coffee, it must be rewritten.

Every engineer, designer, and content contributor must review their work against this document before submitting changes.
